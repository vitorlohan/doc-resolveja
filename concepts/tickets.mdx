A ticket is the fundamental unit of work in Resolve. Every infrastructure issue, maintenance request, or operational task begins as a ticket — a structured record that captures what needs to be done, who is responsible, and how urgent it is. Tickets move through a defined lifecycle on the Kanban board, giving your entire team a shared, real-time view of what's happening across your infrastructure.

## Ticket Anatomy

Each ticket is composed of a set of core fields. Understanding what each field means helps your team create clear, actionable tickets that are easy to triage and resolve.

### Title and Description

The **title** is a short, scannable summary of the issue — aim for something specific enough that anyone on the team can understand the problem at a glance (e.g., "Database replica lag exceeding 30s on prod-db-02" rather than "DB issue").

The **description** gives the full picture. Use it to document:
- What is happening and what the expected behavior should be
- Steps to reproduce the issue or context for how it was discovered
- Any initial findings, error messages, or relevant logs
- Links to runbooks, dashboards, or related tickets

### Status

Status reflects where a ticket currently sits in your workflow. Every ticket moves through statuses as work progresses.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **To Do** | The ticket has been created and is waiting to be picked up. |
| **In Progress** | Someone is actively working on the issue. |
| **In Review** | The fix or change has been applied and is under review or verification. |
| **Done** | The work is complete and verified. The ticket is resolved. |
| **Closed** | The ticket has been formally closed — either resolved, duplicate, or no longer relevant. |

### Priority

Priority tells your team how urgently a ticket needs attention. Setting the right priority helps everyone triage their work queue and ensures critical issues get handled first.

<Info>
  When in doubt, lean toward a lower priority — it's easier to escalate a ticket than to create alert fatigue by over-prioritizing. Reserve **Critical** for situations that are actively impacting production systems or end users.
</Info>

| Priority | When to Use |
|---|---|
| **Critical** | Active outage or severe degradation affecting production systems or users right now. Requires immediate response. |
| **High** | Significant issue that will escalate if not addressed soon, but systems are still partially functional. |
| **Medium** | Notable problem that should be addressed in the current cycle, but is not immediately blocking work. |
| **Low** | Minor issue, cosmetic problem, or improvement that can be scheduled for a future sprint. |

<Info>
  Critical tickets automatically trigger escalation notifications to on-call personnel. Make sure your on-call schedule is configured under **Settings → On-Call** so the right people are alerted.
</Info>

### Category

Categories group tickets by the part of your infrastructure they relate to. This makes it easier to filter the board and assign tickets to the right team or specialist.

- **Network** — Connectivity issues, firewall rules, VPN, DNS, load balancers
- **Server** — Hardware faults, OS-level issues, capacity, provisioning
- **Database** — Query performance, replication lag, backups, schema changes
- **Application** — Bugs, deployment failures, service errors, API issues
- **Facilities** — Physical infrastructure, power, cooling, data center access

Your administrator can add or rename categories to match your organization's structure.

### Assignee and Reporter

- **Assignee** — The person responsible for resolving the ticket. A ticket can only have one assignee at a time, ensuring clear ownership. You can reassign a ticket at any point as work evolves.
- **Reporter** — The person who opened the ticket. Resolve automatically sets this to the logged-in user who created the ticket. The reporter receives notifications when the ticket's status changes.

### Due Date

The due date communicates when the ticket should be resolved. Resolve uses this field to:
- Sort and filter tickets by urgency on the board
- Send automated reminders as the deadline approaches
- Flag overdue tickets with a visual indicator and escalation notification

### Tags and Labels

Tags are freeform labels you can attach to any ticket for additional context. Use them to group related tickets, mark tickets for a specific sprint or incident, or flag tickets for follow-up. Unlike categories, tags are flexible and team-defined.

Examples: `incident-2024-09`, `post-mortem`, `needs-runbook`, `compliance`

### Comments and Attachments

The comment thread on a ticket is the canonical log of work activity. Use comments to:
- Post status updates and findings
- Ask questions and tag teammates with `@mentions`
- Document decisions made during resolution

Attachments let you upload screenshots, log files, configuration diffs, or any supporting files directly to the ticket so all context stays in one place.

## Ticket Lifecycle

A ticket moves through a predictable lifecycle from the moment it is created to the moment it is closed.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Creation">
    A team member (or an automated integration) opens a ticket, fills in the title, description, category, and priority, and optionally sets a due date. The ticket enters the **To Do** column.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assignment">
    A team lead or the ticket creator assigns the ticket to the appropriate engineer. The assignee receives an instant notification. The ticket remains in **To Do** until work begins.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Work Begins">
    The assignee picks up the ticket and moves it to **In Progress**. They use the comment thread to log findings, post updates, and communicate blockers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review">
    Once the fix or change is applied, the assignee moves the ticket to **In Review**. A peer or team lead verifies the resolution — running tests, checking dashboards, or confirming with the reporter that the issue is resolved.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Resolution">
    After verification, the ticket moves to **Done**. The reporter and any watchers are notified automatically. The resolution can be documented in a final comment before the ticket is closed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Closure">
    The ticket is moved to **Closed**, marking it as part of the historical record. Closed tickets are searchable and can be referenced when similar issues arise in the future.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  You can reopen a **Closed** ticket if the same issue recurs. Reopening preserves the full comment history, making it easier to understand what was tried before.
</Info>

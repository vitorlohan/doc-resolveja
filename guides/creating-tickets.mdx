Every infrastructure issue, maintenance task, or outage response starts with a well-formed ticket. Creating a ticket in Resolve puts your work on the shared Kanban board, triggers the right notifications for your team, and gives everyone a single source of truth to track progress from discovery to resolution.

## Create a New Ticket

<Steps>
  <Step title="Click 'New Ticket'">
    From anywhere in Resolve, click the **New Ticket** button in the top-right corner of the screen. A creation panel will slide in from the right side without navigating you away from your current view.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write a descriptive title">
    Enter a clear, specific title that summarizes the issue in plain language. A good title lets teammates immediately understand the problem without opening the ticket. Aim for 8–12 words that include the affected system and the nature of the issue.

    | ❌ Vague | ✅ Descriptive |
    |---|---|
    | Server down | Production DB server unresponsive — EU-West-1 |
    | Network issue | Office VPN dropping connections intermittently |
    | Fix API | Payment API returning 503 after deploy v2.4.1 |
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a detailed description">
    Use the description field to provide everything a responder needs to act without asking follow-up questions. A strong description includes:

    - What you observed (symptoms, error messages, logs)
    - When the issue started or was detected
    - What is affected (systems, users, services)
    - What you have already tried
    - Any relevant links, runbook references, or dashboards
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select a category">
    Choose the category that best matches the affected area of infrastructure. Categories are used for filtering, routing, and reporting.

    <CardGroup cols={3}>
      <Card title="Network" icon="network-wired">
        Connectivity, routing, firewall, DNS, VPN, and bandwidth issues.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Server" icon="server">
        Physical or virtual machines, OS-level problems, resource exhaustion.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Database" icon="database">
        Query performance, replication lag, schema changes, backups.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Application" icon="code">
        Service errors, deploy failures, API degradation, configuration issues.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Facilities" icon="building">
        Data center, power, cooling, physical access, hardware replacements.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Other" icon="ellipsis">
        Anything that doesn't fit the categories above.
      </Card>
    </CardGroup>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the priority level">
    Priority determines urgency, SLA targets, and who gets notified immediately. Choose honestly — overusing Critical degrades its meaning for the whole team.

    <Accordion title="Critical">
      A live outage or severe degradation affecting production systems, customers, or business operations **right now**. Use Critical only when immediate action is required and the issue cannot wait. Triggers instant notifications to the on-call team.
    </Accordion>
    <Accordion title="High">
      A significant problem that needs same-day attention. Production is affected but a workaround exists, or the issue is likely to escalate soon without action.
    </Accordion>
    <Accordion title="Medium">
      A real issue that should be addressed this week. No immediate customer impact, but it carries risk if left unattended.
    </Accordion>
    <Accordion title="Low">
      A known issue, improvement, or maintenance task with no time pressure. Useful for tracking work that would otherwise get lost.
    </Accordion>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assign to a team member">
    Select an owner from the **Assignee** dropdown to make one person accountable for the ticket. You can also leave the ticket unassigned — it will appear in the **Unassigned** lane on the Kanban board so anyone can pick it up. You can always reassign later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set a due date (optional)">
    Add a due date if the work has a hard deadline — a maintenance window, a compliance requirement, or a customer commitment. Resolve will send automated reminders as the date approaches.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add tags (optional)">
    Tags are free-form labels that help with search and filtering beyond categories. Common examples include environment names (`production`, `staging`), team names (`infra-team`, `dba`), incident IDs, or project codes. You can add multiple tags per ticket.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit the ticket">
    Click **Create Ticket**. The ticket appears immediately on the Kanban board in the **To Do** column and all configured notifications are dispatched. You will land on the new ticket's detail view where you can continue adding files or comments.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Writing Good Titles and Descriptions

The quality of your ticket directly affects how fast it gets resolved. Use these guidelines every time you open a ticket.

**For titles:**
- Lead with the affected system or service, then describe what is wrong
- Avoid vague verbs like "issue," "problem," or "fix" alone — add specifics
- Include environment context when it matters: `[PROD]`, `[STAGING]`
- Keep it under 80 characters so it doesn't truncate in list views

**For descriptions:**
- Write for someone who has never seen this issue before
- Paste actual error messages rather than paraphrasing them
- Link to relevant monitoring dashboards, logs, or runbooks
- Include reproduction steps for application-level bugs
- State the business impact — "X customers cannot check out" is more actionable than "checkout is broken"

## Attaching Files and Screenshots

Visual evidence dramatically speeds up diagnosis. To attach files to a ticket:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the ticket detail view">
    Click on any ticket to open its full detail panel.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Navigate to the Attachments section">
    Scroll to the **Attachments** section below the description, or find the paperclip icon in the toolbar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload your files">
    Drag and drop files directly onto the attachment area, or click **Browse Files** to select from your device. Supported formats include PNG, JPG, GIF, PDF, CSV, and plain text log files. Maximum file size is 25 MB per attachment.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can also paste screenshots directly into the **description** or **comment** fields using `Ctrl+V` / `Cmd+V` — Resolve will upload the image automatically and embed it inline.

<Tip>
  Save time on recurring issues by using **ticket templates**. Your admin can create templates pre-filled with standard titles, descriptions, categories, and tags for common issue types — scheduled maintenance windows, certificate renewals, and backup failures are good candidates. Select a template from the **New Ticket** panel by clicking **Use Template** before filling in any fields.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  Setting a ticket to **Critical** priority triggers immediate push and email notifications to every member of the on-call rotation, regardless of their personal notification settings. Use Critical only for genuine production emergencies. Misusing Critical priority causes alert fatigue and trains your team to ignore urgent pages.
</Warning>

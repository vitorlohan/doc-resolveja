This page covers organization-wide notification rules — a feature available exclusively to Resolve Admins. While individual team members can manage their own personal notification preferences, notification rules let you define system-level alerts that fire automatically for the right people regardless of any individual's personal settings. Use them to ensure critical infrastructure events are never missed.

## What Notification Rules Are

Notification rules are standing instructions that Resolve evaluates continuously against all ticket activity. When a ticket event matches a rule's conditions, Resolve immediately sends an alert to the configured recipients through the chosen channel. Unlike personal preferences, these rules operate at the organization level and cannot be suppressed by individual users.

<Note>
  Notification rules are separate from personal notification preferences. A rule-triggered alert will be sent even if the recipient has turned off notifications for that event type in their personal settings.
</Note>

## Rule Anatomy

Every notification rule is made up of three components:

- **Trigger condition** — The event and filter criteria that activate the rule (e.g., "a ticket is created" + "priority = Critical")
- **Recipient(s)** — Who receives the alert: a specific user, a role (all Admins), or a team (the On-Call team)
- **Channel** — How the alert is delivered: via email or as an in-app notification

Together, these three components define exactly *when* an alert fires, *who* it reaches, and *how* they receive it.

## Creating a New Notification Rule

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to Settings → Notification Rules">
    From the main navigation, click **Settings**, then select **Notification Rules** from the left sidebar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click 'Add Rule'">
    Click the **+ Add Rule** button in the top-right corner of the Notification Rules page. A rule configuration panel will open.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the trigger">
    Choose an **Event Type** from the dropdown. Available events include:

    - Ticket created
    - Ticket status changed
    - Ticket assigned
    - Comment added
    - Ticket overdue
    - Ticket priority changed

    Then add one or more **Conditions** to narrow when the rule fires (e.g., Priority = Critical, Category = Network, Overdue by = 24 hours).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the recipients">
    Choose who receives the alert. You can select:

    - **Specific users** — search by name or email
    - **By role** — All Admins, All Members
    - **By team** — any team you've configured under Settings → Teams
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the channel">
    Select one or both delivery channels:

    - **Email** — sends a formatted email to the recipient's registered address
    - **In-app** — displays a notification in the Resolve notification bell
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the rule">
    Give your rule a descriptive name (e.g., "Critical ticket — ops alert"), then click **Save Rule**. The rule becomes active immediately.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Use descriptive rule names that explain the trigger and the audience — for example, "Server ticket created → Admins (in-app)". This makes your rules list much easier to audit and maintain over time.
</Tip>

## Managing Existing Rules

From the **Notification Rules** list, you can perform the following actions on any rule:

- **Edit** — Click the **pencil icon** to modify any part of the rule. Changes take effect immediately on save.
- **Disable** — Toggle the rule off without deleting it. Disabled rules are grayed out in the list and will not fire until re-enabled.
- **Delete** — Click the **trash icon** to permanently remove a rule. This action cannot be undone.

<Tip>
  When testing a new rule configuration, disable it temporarily rather than deleting it. This lets you re-enable it quickly if needed without rebuilding it from scratch.
</Tip>

## Rule Priority and Conflict Resolution

When multiple rules match the same ticket event, Resolve fires all matching rules — recipients may receive more than one notification for a single event. Rules do not cancel or override each other.

To avoid redundant alerts:

- Use specific conditions to narrow each rule's scope
- Consolidate rules that target the same audience with similar triggers
- Regularly audit your rules list and remove or merge overlapping rules

<Warning>
  Too many notification rules — especially broad ones with no conditions — will flood your team's inboxes and in-app feeds. When recipients start ignoring or filtering out Resolve alerts, you lose the early-warning value that notification rules are meant to provide. Audit your rules regularly and favor targeted, high-signal rules over broad catch-all ones.
</Warning>
